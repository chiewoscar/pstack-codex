### Autopilot-full

**Prerequisite:** This playbook assumes the repository uses Graphite and the `gt` CLI. Check both before applying Graphite commands. Follow the user's approval boundary for pushes and merges. If Graphite is absent, use the repository's own review workflow and omit Graphite-specific steps.

**You own the verdicts, never the PRs. One owner runs each PR from build to merge, and nothing merges without your clean swarm verdict.** For "autopilot this queue", "full autopilot", and one-owner-per-PR programs. The job is a queue of independent PRs handed over to drive to merged with full autonomy. Orchestrate runs a standing program whose coordinator lands verified work itself and whose workers never merge; here each PR's owner carries the whole lifecycle through the merge, and the root keeps only verification, countersigns, and audits.

1. **Mark the operator's items and honor state-then-wait.** Items the operator names stay hers. She reviews and she clicks, and no owner merges one. When she asks for the protocol or the plan to be stated, deliver the statement and stop. Execution starts only on her explicit go.
2. **Spawn one owner per PR with the full lifecycle.** One authorized Codex worker with its own worktree per PR owns build, gt registration, self-proof on the real artifact (the **prove-it-works** principle skill), skeptical Bugbot triage per `../references/bugbot-triage.md`, a slop-strip (the **deslop** skill (`$pstack-codex:deslop`)), `$pstack-codex:no-comments` (the **no-comments** skill), an initial restack onto current trunk, the babysit loop to green (`playbooks/babysit.md`), and the merge itself. The initial restack precedes babysit; merge prep in step 5 checks trunk again. Every owner keeps a decisions.tsv trail per the **show-me-your-work** skill, never committed, returned with its reports. The merge is the one step an owner may not take alone; step 4 gates it.
3. **Run owners in true parallel and never stack.** Many owners at once when PRs are self-contained: one writer per branch, disjoint files, cross-PR drift absorbed by rebase. Only genuinely overlapping work serializes. Self-contained PRs branch straight off main, and sequenced work is merge-then-branch. Every PR is still gt-registered; the Graphite-metadata rule is about the UI, not stacks. One exception: an owner that must split a genuinely dependent change may hold a short private stack.
4. **Swarm-verify every changed patch before its merge.** Start a verification round at the owner's code-ready head and start a new round after every push that changes the patch. Fan out independent verifiers per the **swarm** skill when the active host and task allow delegation, then aggregate to one verdict. The lanes: run the relevant gates at that SHA; prove the load-bearing behavior on the real surface (the project's `verify-*` skill when available); audit the receipts and diff against trunk, distrusting the PR body. Give review lanes distinct focuses, such as caller parity, races, or configuration safety. Send proven findings together for one fix-forward; ask for a red test or a reproducible receipt. The new patch needs a new verdict. The live lane is the floor, and a verdict without it is not clean. No merge without the root's clean verdict.
5. **On a clean verdict the owner merges and takes the next item.** Merge prep rebases or restacks onto current trunk only after the review lanes have started. Record the resulting head SHA and wait for CI on that exact head. The patch-id rule in `playbooks/shipping.md` decides whether the earlier verdict still applies; a changed patch needs a new round. The owner squash-merges its own PR and picks up its next self-contained item from the queue only when the operator's full-autonomy grant and the root's clean verdict authorize it. Operator-named items stop at merge-ready and wait for the operator.
6. **Run the root layer.** A genuinely new raise of a pinned gate or budget value (a limit CI only lets tighten) needs your fresh countersign, granted only after verifier proof. Absorbing values that already landed on main is drift, not a raise. During the active task, check owner progress at bounded intervals using collaboration status tools. Schedule a Codex heartbeat only if the user explicitly requested recurring monitoring. Each check audits progress and protocol adherence and collects decision trails. When merges batch, run a retro pass and a post-merge bot-comment sweep.
7. **Stand down instantly on the operator's stop.** Her hold or stand-down reaches every owner as a zero-writes order immediately. Owners hold their briefs until she releases them.

**Reply:** the queue with each PR's owner, state, and head SHA; each verdict and the swarm that produced it; what merged and what each owner took next; countersigns granted and why; open operator gates; where the collected decision trails live.
